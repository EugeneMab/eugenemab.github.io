Franstant (Francais Constant)

An artificial language built upon French but without conjugation by Jian Huang

1. Introduction

Welcome to Franstant. It is an artificial language built upon French, but without conjugation. The design goal is to simplify grammar while retaining the core vocabulary and structure of French. Verbs do not change, and nouns remain in a single masculine singular form, eliminating irregularities through uniform rules.

2. Nouns, Pronouns, and Possession

All nouns strictly use the base masculine singular form. There are no irregular plurals; instead, plurality is indicated solely by the article.
Example: un oeil -> les oeil (not yeux); un cheval -> des cheval (not chevaux).

Pronouns always follow Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) order. Object pronouns do not move before the verb. Possession uses the masculine possessive plus the plural indicator 'les'.
Example: I see him. -> moi voir lui; My friends. -> mon les ami.

3. Verbs and Negation

Verbs are not conjugated and always remain in the infinitive. The negation 'ne pas' is kept together and placed directly before the verb without splitting.
Example: I do not eat. -> moi ne pas manger; Did you go to the restaurant? -> être ce que toi passé aller à le restaurant ?

4. Adjectives and Articles

Adjectives are always placed after the noun. Articles and pronoun possession remain at the beginning of the noun phrase. There is no elision (no apostrophes) between words.
Example: The smart man. -> le homme intelligent; My beautiful children. -> mon les enfant beau.

For the partitive 'some', Franstant introduces 'du'. However, to express 'of the', 'de le' is strictly used without combining.
Example: I eat bread (some bread). -> moi manger du pain; The roof of the house. -> le toit de le maison.

5. Schemas and Pronunciation

The letters follow standard French, but the pronunciation strictly follows spelling without elision. For example, 'le annee' is pronounced as three distinct syllables. Because elision is removed, the pronunciation intentionally deviates from traditional French rhythm to ensure each word is clearly identifiable.
