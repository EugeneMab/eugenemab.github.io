Franstant (Français Constant)

An artificial language built upon French without conjugation — by Jian Huang

1. Introduction

Franstant (Français Constant) is an artificial language built upon French. "Constant" means that every word keeps an invariable form — verbs do not conjugate, nouns do not take plural forms, adjectives do not agree in gender or number. The goal is to radically simplify grammar while retaining the vocabulary, phonetics, and cultural spirit of French.

The core design principles are:

1. Constant verbs: Verbs always remain in the infinitive. Tense is expressed through markers (passé = past, futur = future, être = continuous).
2. Constant nouns: Nouns stay in the base masculine singular form. Plurality is indicated only by the article.
3. No contractions: All elisions and contractions are removed. Every word is written in full.
4. Fixed word order: Strict Subject-Verb-Object order. Adjectives are always placed after the noun.
5. Unsplit negation: "ne pas" appears together before the verb.
6. Pronunciation adapts to spelling: Pronunciation follows the new spelling without elision.

2. Detailed Rules and Examples

2.1 Articles

Definite articles reduce to le (singular) and les (plural). "la" and "l'" are removed. Indefinite articles reduce to un (singular) and des (plural). "une" is removed. The partitive article is uniformly du (meaning "some"). Note that de le is reserved for "of the" (possession) and never contracts to "du".

All nouns strictly use the base masculine singular form. There are no irregular plurals. Plurality is indicated only by the articles "les" or "des" — the noun itself never changes.

2.5 Verbs and Tense

Verbs always remain in the infinitive without any conjugation. Tense is expressed through markers placed before the verb.

Tense Marker Standard French Franstant
Present (none) Je mange.moi manger.
Past passé J'ai mangé.moi passé manger.
Future futur Je mangerai.moi futur manger.
Near Future aller Je vais manger.moi aller manger.
Continuous être Je suis en train de manger.moi être manger.

2.6 Negation

The negation ne pas is placed as a single block before the verb (or tense marker), without splitting.

Examples
Je ne mange pas. → moi ne pas manger.
Il n'est pas venu. → lui ne pas passé venir.
Tu ne partiras pas. → toi ne pas futur partir.

2.7 Adjectives

Adjectives are fixed to the masculine singular form and always placed after the noun. Articles and possessives remain at the beginning of the noun phrase.

Standard French Franstant
le beau garçonle garçon beau
une belle maisonun maison beau
les grandes villesles ville grand
mes beaux enfantsmon les enfant beau
la plus belle femmele femme le plus beau

2.8 Prepositions

Prepositions never contract with articles. All contracted forms are written separately.

Standard French Franstant
au restaurantà le restaurant
aux États-Unisà les État-Uni
du pain (of the bread)de le pain
d'eaude le eau

2.9 Questions

Questions uniformly use être ce que. "Est-ce que" is not used (because "est" is conjugated), nor are hyphens or elisions.

Examples
Est-ce que tu vas au restaurant ? → être ce que toi aller à le restaurant ?
Est-ce qu'il est ici ? → être ce que lui être ici ?

2.10 Passive Voice

The passive voice is expressed by the marker passif. Past passive uses passé passif.

Examples
Le livre est lu. → le livre passif lire.
La maison a été construite par son père. → le maison passé passif construire par son père.

2.11 Reflexive Verbs

All reflexive pronouns are unified to se, placed before the verb.

Examples
Je me lave. → moi se laver.
Ils se trouvent là-bas. → eux se trouver là.

2.12 Existence

Il y a is uniformly replaced by voilà.

Il y a beaucoup d'enfants. → voilà beaucoup de enfant.

Stress

As in French, the stress always falls on the last syllable of the rhythmic group.

Differences from Standard French

In standard French, when a word ending in a vowel meets a word beginning with a vowel, elision occurs (like l'homme, d'eau) and liaison happens (like les amis pronounced [le.za.mi]). In Franstant, these phenomena do not exist. Each word is pronounced independently: "le homme" is pronounced [lə.ɔm], "de le eau" is pronounced [də.lə.o]. While this creates hiatus, it is a deliberate design choice of Franstant to ensure clear word boundaries.
