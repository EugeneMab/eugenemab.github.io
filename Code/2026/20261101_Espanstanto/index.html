Espanstanto (español constanto)

An artificial language built upon Spanish without conjugation

1. Introduction

Espanstanto is an artificial language built upon Spanish without conjugation. It follows the design of Franstant, applied to Spanish.

Every verb stays in the infinitive. Tense, mood, and voice are shown by a marker in front of the verb.

Markers are real forms of HACER, never invented words. The main verb never changes.

2. Design principles

  • Spanish vocabulary, zero conjugation: every verb is the infinitive plus a marker.
  • No contractions: a el, never al; de el, never del. No elisions.
  • Strict SVO word order.
  • No grammatical gender: el and un for every noun.
  • Nouns keep the masculine-singular form; plurality comes from the article only: el/los, un/unos.
  • Adjectives are always masculine-singular and always after the noun.
  • Possessives use the masculine form; a plural possessed thing is possessive + los + noun.
  • One standalone pronoun set; word order decides subject or object. No mí/ti.
  • Lo is banned; use el instead.
  • Demonstratives stay separate: este/ese/aquel; plurals add los.
  • Esto is a pronoun; este is an adjective.
  • Relatives use que only; word order decides subject or object. Quien is dropped.
  • Ser and estar stay separate, always in the infinitive.
  • Hay stays hay.
  • The a before a human direct object is kept.

4. Grammar

  • Articles: el/los, un/unos. Write a el, never al; de el, never del.
  • Pronouns: yo, tú, él, nosotros, vosotros, ellos; inanimate ello/ellos. Position decides subject or object.
  • Demonstratives: este/ese/aquel are adjectives; esto/eso/aquello are pronouns; plurals add los.
  • Relatives: que only; donde for where; de que for whose.
  • Comparatives: más/menos/tan + adjective + que; el más/el menos; invariable.
  • Negation: no stands unsplit before the verb or marker. Say no ver nada, no ver nunca.
  • Questions: ser que for yes/no; qué + SVO for what.
  • Numbers: diez; diez uno…diez nueve; compounds drop y (veinte uno); setenta/ochenta/noventa; cien/mil invariable.
  • Ser and estar stay separate, always in the infinitive. The passive uses ser (es hecho), never estar.
  • Passive: es hecho in every tense; the tense marker stacks in front.
  • Pronominal: se + infinitive. Near future: ir a + infinitive. Continuous: está haciendo + infinitive.
  • Hay stays hay. Keep the a before a human direct object. Quotation marks « » are kept.

5. Examples

Each example shows all four languages.

English: I want to eat.
中文: 我想吃东西。
Español: Quiero comer.
